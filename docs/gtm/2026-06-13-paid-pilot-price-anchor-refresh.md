# 2026-06-13 Paid Pilot Price Anchor Refresh

Status: GTM refresh note  
Date: 2026-06-13  
Owner: Orvo N2 Pro GTM / Pricing / Packaging

## Executive recommendation

Keep the paid pilot anchored at **USD 149 / 30 days**, but tighten the follow-on packaging around a clearer public price ladder:

- **Pilot / Activation Sprint:** USD 149 for 30 days, 1 Tiendanube store, daily owner/operator brief, case queue, evidence-backed cases, and a weekly review.
- **Starter:** USD 99 / mo public price after pilot, with a limited design-partner window at USD 79 / mo for the first 3-5 stores.
- **Growth:** USD 199 / mo once the case workflow and one additional connector, likely Meta Ads, are ready.
- **Custom:** scope only when the store exceeds the Starter/Growth limits or needs multi-store / agency / custom integration work.

This keeps Orvo above cheap app-store utilities and below managed-service retainers, while staying aligned with the current Tiendanube plan stack.

The homepage also advertises **annual -25%**, **7-day free trial**, **+400 apps**, and **+2000 specialists**.

Implication:
- Orvo should not be priced like a Tiendanube app-store add-on.
- The right comparison is **operational monitoring + follow-up**, not a single utility.
- A store paying ARS 79k/mo for Tiendanube Impulso can rationally pay USD 99/mo for Orvo if Orvo prevents one stockout, one fulfillment miss, or one wasted ad day.

### 2. WhatsApp cost is now measurable, but still small

This is useful for COGS planning, not for customer-facing price justification. Orvo should treat WhatsApp as a **projection channel** and keep daily owner briefs short, because even one message per day can become a real cost at scale.

### 3. The buyer still wants a control plane, not a bot

The strongest GTM framing remains:

> Orvo is the daily operating queue for a Tiendanube store: what needs attention, why it matters, what evidence supports it, and what should happen next.

Avoid:
- “chatbot”
- “dashboard”
- “AI analyst”
- “automate everything”
- “ERP replacement”

Use:
- “control operativo”
- “cola de casos”
- “evidencia”
- “datos stale”
- “qué necesita atención hoy”

## Recommended packaging

### Pilot / Activation Sprint — USD 149 / 30 days

Best for:
- owner-operated Tiendanube stores
- 100-500 orders/month
- 50-300 active SKUs
- daily WhatsApp-first operations
- owner or operator already checking sales, stock, shipping, and chats manually

Includes:
- 1 Tiendanube store
- 1 daily owner/operator brief
- case queue / history
- data freshness checks
- stock risk setup
- sales floor setup
- readiness review for fulfillment, customer attention, fiscal/admin, and treasury/reporting
- 1 weekly review
- manual operator-assisted setup and tuning

Success criteria:
- Orvo opens or updates useful cases with evidence
- the brief replaces at least one manual check
- the merchant can point to one avoided miss, one saved hour, or one clearer decision
- the merchant is willing to continue on Starter or Growth

### Starter — USD 99 / mo public, USD 79 / mo design-partner window

Best for:
- small D2C stores that want a daily operating queue without agency-level complexity

Includes:
- Tiendanube connector
- daily brief
- case queue / history
- stock risk
- sales floor
- data freshness
- basic operator workflow
- 2 recipients
- 90 days history
- 500 orders/month soft limit

Do not include:
- Meta Ads
- custom connectors
- agency portfolio view
- autonomous actions
- guaranteed revenue lift

### Growth — USD 199 / mo

Best for:
- stores with enough complexity that missed signals are expensive
- more than ~500 orders/month
- more than ~300 active SKUs
- active ad spend
- multiple operators
- fulfillment or WISMO pressure
- agency-managed operations

Includes:
- everything in Starter
- more recipients
- more case workflow depth
- weekly review
- stronger freshness monitoring
- one additional standard connector when ready, likely Meta Ads
- fulfillment backlog once the readiness gate passes

### Custom

Use only when the store exceeds the normal product limits:
- multiple stores
- custom connector
- agency portfolio
- formal SLA
- security / procurement requirements

## ICP refinement

### Best-fit profile

- Tiendanube store in Argentina or LatAm
- owner or operator already uses WhatsApp as the daily operating surface
- store has enough order volume that one missed issue matters
- manual checks are spread across Tiendanube, WhatsApp, shipping, ads, and spreadsheets
- buyer values “what needs attention today” more than raw dashboards

### Strong lead signals

- owner asks “qué pasó hoy?” across sales, stock, shipping, and chats
- they already pay for Tiendanube Impulso or Escala
- they run paid ads or have a meaningful ad budget
- they have recurring stockouts, delayed dispatch, or unanswered chats
- they have an operator or agency that needs visibility
- they are already using WhatsApp as a sales and support channel

### Weak-fit signals

- free Tiendanube plan with very low order volume
- no WhatsApp usage
- no operator or owner time constraint
- wants only a chatbot or only a dashboard
- wants guaranteed revenue lift
- wants ERP replacement or full accounting automation

## Buyer objections and replies

### “Ya tengo Tiendanube”

Tiendanube is the commerce engine. Orvo is the operating queue above it.

Suggested reply:
> Tiendanube te muestra la tienda. Orvo te muestra qué necesita atención hoy, con evidencia y seguimiento.

### “Ya tengo dashboards”

Dashboards show metrics. Orvo turns metrics into cases.

Suggested reply:
> El dashboard te dice qué pasó. Orvo te dice qué está abierto, qué está stale, y qué hacer ahora.

### “Ya uso WhatsApp / Zoko / WATI”

Those tools manage conversations. Orvo uses WhatsApp as the alert surface for operational cases.

Suggested reply:
> El WhatsApp sigue siendo el canal. Orvo convierte lo operativo en casos y te avisa por WhatsApp cuando hay algo que atender.

### “¿Y si el dato está mal?”

That is exactly where Orvo earns trust.

Suggested reply:
> Si el dato no es confiable, Orvo lo marca como stale y no inventa certezas.

### “¿Es caro para Argentina?”

Use the Tiendanube plan anchor and the value of one prevented miss.

Suggested reply:
> Si una tienda paga ARS 79k/mo por Tiendanube Impulso, USD 99/mo por Orvo se justifica con un solo stockout evitado o una hora diaria de chequeos manuales recuperada.

## ROI model

Use a simple break-even model:

- **Monthly value** = avoided stockout margin + saved manual check time + prevented fulfillment/WISMO pain + reduced ad waste
- **Break-even** = Orvo monthly price / gross margin per avoided incident
- **Pilot success** = at least one useful case or one manual check replaced

Example framing:
> If Orvo prevents one missed stockout or one delayed dispatch per month, the Sprint usually pays for itself.

## Demo script

1. Start with the morning chaos: Tiendanube, WhatsApp, stock, shipping, ads, spreadsheets.
2. Show the OS snapshot: what is fresh, what is stale, what is open.
3. Open one real-style case: sales drop, stock risk, or data stale.
4. Show evidence and the next action.
5. Show the WhatsApp brief as a projection, not the product.
6. Show follow-up memory: open, acknowledged, resolved, dismissed.
7. Close with the Activation Sprint and the 30-day success criteria.

Do not demo fictional autonomous actions, invoice issuance, reconciliation, or customer auto-replies.
